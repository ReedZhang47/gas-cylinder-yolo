and it is checked rather than asserted (Table~\ref{tab:independence}): similarity is measured

The three arms differ only in how the 93 seed photographs are expanded; the training recipe,
the snapshot schedule and the evaluation are shared. Because the same 61 development images
both choose a checkpoint and report a score, the numbers are separated by role rather than
merged: a fixed endpoint, a cross-fitted pooled out-of-fold estimate, and a separately
identified deployment choice, exactly as pre-registered. Figure~\ref{fig:ci} gives the
arm-level contrasts with paired cluster-bootstrap intervals, together with the same contrasts
computed detector by detector; Table~\ref{tab:main} lists every detector and
Table~\ref{tab:nested-selection} gives the arm-level selection.

Two readings matter. First, the edited-synthesis arm improves on both the real-only and the
classical-augmentation arms, and both intervals stay clear of zero. Second, the
classical-augmentation arm is not distinguishable from the real-only arm at the arm level:
expanding 93 photographs to 1085 by geometric and colour transforms buys no detectable gain.
That second reading is an arm-level statement, not a per-detector one --- the per-detector
panels show the augmented arm significantly ahead at two of the six detectors and never
significantly behind, which is exactly why the arm-level estimate, rather than a
best-detector score, is what gets reported.

\subsection{Data-scale ablation}\label{sec:scale}

The synthesis arm is the only arm whose data can be scaled cheaply, so it carries the
data-size experiment: the identical recipe is trained on the first 493 edited images and on
all 1085. Every reported quantity rises with the pool --- the pooled out-of-fold estimate
under the primary metric, the same estimate under mAP50, and the deployment score
(Figure~\ref{fig:scale} and Table~\ref{tab:scale}). The paired cluster bootstrap puts the step
clear of zero under both metrics (Table~\ref{tab:bootstrap}), so this is a detectable gain
rather than an artefact of which checkpoint the folds happened to pick. It is not uniformly
detectable detector by detector --- every per-detector point estimate is positive, but only
some of the intervals clear zero --- so the scale effect is an arm-level finding on 61
evaluation images, not a claim about any single detector. A larger pool also steadies the
selection rule: at 493 images the five folds divide between two detectors and the deployment
choice lands on \texttt{yolo26m}, whereas at 1085 images they agree on \texttt{yolo26s}.

\subsection{Checkpoint-selection stability}\label{sec:stability}
% 当前只有 A 臂结果；B/C 齐后补同一张表的两行，并核对稳定性结论是否在 1085 张上复现。
Each detector is trained once and snapshotted every ten epochs, so the reporting protocol
has to choose a checkpoint. The pre-registered rule smooths the dev61 mAP50--95 curve over
three adjacent snapshots and takes its maximum, resolving ties towards the earlier epoch.
Figure~\ref{fig:selection} shows the curves behind that rule, arm by arm, marking for each
detector the epoch selected on all of dev61 (the deployment choice) and the epoch selected
by each of the five cross-fitting folds; Table~\ref{tab:selection} lists those choices
together with the resulting scores.

The fixed-endpoint and pooled-OOF columns must not be read as two models scored on one test
set. The fixed endpoint is a single checkpoint evaluated on all of dev61, whereas the pooled
estimate mixes five fold-selected checkpoints, each scored only on the images that were
excluded when it was chosen. What the pair shows is whether the selection rule earns its
keep. On arm~A it does not do so uniformly: for four of the six detectors the pooled
estimate falls slightly below the fixed endpoint, while for the two strongest detectors it
improves on it substantially (Table~\ref{tab:selection}). Selection stability is also itself
informative. The five folds agree exactly for \texttt{yolov8s} and \texttt{yolo26s}, whereas
\texttt{yolo11m} selects a different epoch depending on the fold, and that detector is the
least reliable on this arm in every other respect as well.
