\subsection{Checkpoint-selection stability}\label{sec:stability}
% 当前只有 A 臂结果；B/C 齐后补同一张表的两行，并核对稳定性结论是否在 1085 张上复现。
Each detector is trained once and snapshotted every ten epochs, so the reporting protocol
has to choose a checkpoint. The pre-registered rule smooths the dev61 mAP50--95 curve over
three adjacent snapshots and takes its maximum, resolving ties towards the earlier epoch.
Figure~\ref{fig:selection} shows the curves behind that rule for arm~A, marking for each
detector the epoch selected on all of dev61 (the deployment choice) and the epoch selected
by each of the five cross-fitting folds; Table~\ref{tab:selection} lists those choices
together with the resulting scores.

The fixed-endpoint and pooled-OOF columns must not be read as two models scored on one test
set. The fixed endpoint is a single checkpoint evaluated on all of dev61, whereas the pooled
estimate mixes five fold-selected checkpoints, each scored only on the images that were
excluded when it was chosen. What the pair shows is whether the selection rule earns its
keep. On this arm it does not do so uniformly: for four of the six detectors the pooled
estimate falls slightly below the fixed endpoint, while for the two strongest detectors it
improves on it substantially (Table~\ref{tab:selection}). Selection stability is also itself
informative. The five folds agree exactly for \texttt{yolov8s} and \texttt{yolo26s}, whereas
\texttt{yolo11m} selects a different epoch depending on the fold, and that detector is the
least reliable on this arm in every other respect as well.
